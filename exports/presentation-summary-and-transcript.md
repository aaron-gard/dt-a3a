## 1. Written summary

### The brief
Capgemini Australia asked the team to address a workforce challenge: how to prepare staff to collaborate effectively with AI in the workplace. Two objectives from the brief:

1. Identify and map the essential human skills required for human–AI co-creation.
2. Design a training program that prepares staff to integrate AI into creative processes.

### How might we
> How might we build a structured AI training program that equips business users with confidence in their critical thinking and technical ability, so that human–AI co-creativity becomes a genuine individual capability?

### Strategic requirements
Any solution must:

1. Provide a **structured training program** so every staff member completes a defined AI training pathway.
2. Build **confidence in critical thinking** so staff can judge, refine and challenge AI outputs.
3. Enable **co-creativity as an individual capability** that shows up in client work.

### Challenges and opportunities
- **Structured training** — no shared AI capability baseline today across staff and practices. Opportunity: define a business-wide standard and lift the floor of AI fluency.
- **Critical thinking** — current trust deficit; staff confidence to judge AI outputs varies. Opportunity: structured refinement builds the confidence to question, challenge and improve AI output.
- **Co-creativity** — AI capability is stuck in training mode and is not yet showing up as an individual skill in client work. Opportunity: make co-creativity visible on every engagement.

### Approach
Double-diamond design thinking process:

- **Diverge — Empathise:** Capgemini brief, Jon Le Gat interview (hosted by Associate Professor Kevin Agres), public-domain research on AI adoption and workforce readiness.
- **Converge — Define:** five individual A2 HMW statements merged into one shared team statement.
- **Diverge — Ideate:** three solution alternatives generated.
- **Converge — Evaluate:** weighted Pugh / decision matrix, scored independently by all five members and averaged.
- **Converge — Recommend:** integrated A + B with C as the scalable delivery channel.

### Three solutions
- **A · AI Centre of Excellence** — standing cross-functional team that owns AI training and enablement, curates practice, and provides ongoing guidance as tools evolve. High impact, high effort, strategic and long-term.
- **B · Bespoke industry-specific training** — co-designed curriculum built around Capgemini's real workflows, delivered as a structured learning pathway rather than generic AI modules. High impact, moderate effort — the sweet spot.
- **C · Expert-led LMS with structured assessment** — video modules and testing hosted on a corporate learning platform. Low effort, lower standalone impact; works as a scalable channel.

### Decision matrix
Criteria drawn directly from the HMW:

- Structured AI training (30%)
- Confidence in critical thinking and technical ability (30%)
- Human–AI co-creativity as an individual capability (40%)

**Solution B** scored highest overall as the most balanced option. **A** scored strongest on co-creativity but weaker on structured training. **C** is efficient to deliver but underperforms on the deeper human outcomes.

### Recommendation
An integrated program combining **A + B**, with **C** used selectively as the scalable delivery channel — moving Capgemini toward treating AI as a co-creative partner within 12 months.

### Implementation — six-month rollout
- **Months 1–2 · Partnership establishment.** Stand up the AI Centre of Excellence with cross-functional leaders. Partner with external AI experts and procure the LMS. Establish baseline performance and productivity KPIs.
- **Months 2–3 · Workflow analysis and curriculum design.** Focus groups with staff to map current bottlenecks and design an industry-specific curriculum addressing real workflows, AI demystification and compliant human–AI co-creation guidance.
- **Months 4–5 · System implementation and staff training.** Launch the LMS, video series and interactive modules. Practical sandbox exercises and tests evaluate both technical ability and critical thinking — prompting, output verification, complex-workflow confidence.
- **Month 6+ · Engagement and continuous improvement.** Internal campaigns celebrating successful human–AI co-creation. Continuous feedback loop into the Centre of Excellence; effectiveness measured against the baseline KPIs.

### Budget
Total estimated budget: **~A$200K**, covering technology (LMS), external expertise, and content creation. Indicative at this stage.

### Why it matters
The recommendation moves Capgemini beyond implementing AI tools toward building a culture of confident human–AI collaboration. It reduces employee hesitation, strengthens critical thinking, and supports safer and more effective AI adoption — aligned with Capgemini's broader vision of human-centred, technology-driven transformation.

> "Together we can give technology the human touch the world needs."
> — Aiman Ezzat, CEO, Capgemini

### 12–13 · Implementation plan & budget — Ash
Thanks, Dave. To successfully transition Capgemini staff from viewing AI as a basic productivity tool to a trusted collaborative partner, we propose a comprehensive six-month implementation plan. This strategy integrates the establishment of an AI Centre of Excellence, the development of a bespoke curriculum, and the deployment of a structured learning management system.

The first two months will focus on partnership establishment — setting up both internal leadership and external expertise. Internally, Capgemini will form an AI Centre of Excellence composed of cross-functional leaders who will own the training and AI enablement strategies across the business. Externally, the Centre of Excellence will partner with industry-leading AI experts and procure a robust learning management system tailored for corporate upskilling. During this phase, the Centre of Excellence will establish baseline performance and productivity metrics, setting clear KPIs to measure improvements in staff's critical thinking and technical capabilities.

During months two and three, the focus will be on workflow analysis and curriculum. Rather than just a generic off-the-shelf solution, the training must be deeply relevant to Capgemini's specific operational menu. The Centre of Excellence and external experts will collaborate with staff through focus groups to analyse current bottlenecks and design an industry-specific training curriculum. This phase ensures the resulting video series and course materials directly address staff concerns, demystify AI, and provide clear, compliant guidance on human–AI co-creation.

Months four and five focus on system implementation and staff training. With the curriculum designed, the program moves to deployment. The structured video series and interactive modules will be launched via the LMS, made accessible to all staff. This phase is critical for building confidence — training will include practical sandbox exercises and tests designed to evaluate both technical ability and critical thinking. Staff will learn how to prompt effectively, verify AI outputs, and confidently rely on AI for complex workflows.

And lastly, from month six onwards, we will focus on internal education and engagement. Training is not a one-off event but a continuous cultural shift. The customers in this context are Capgemini's business users; the Centre of Excellence will drive ongoing internal engagement campaigns to celebrate successful human–AI co-creation events and gather continuous feedback from staff to refine the program. Effectiveness will be continuously measured against the initial KPIs, tracking company-wide productivity to ensure the training translates into genuine individual capability.

To execute the six-month rollout effectively, the following budget has been estimated to cover technology, expertise and content creation. The total estimated budget is a little north of A$200,000, and it's an approximation as of now.
