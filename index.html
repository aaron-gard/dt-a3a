Understanding the problem.

AI marks the Fourth Industrial Revolution. What distinguishes it from previous shifts is a dual transformation — it amplifies productivity and ignites an intelligence revolution that reshapes human roles and the competencies required to thrive.

For Capgemini, the value of AI lies not in replacing people but in augmenting creativity. Merging human intuition with AI's computational power unlocks novel solutions for end users — but only if staff are equipped to lead that integration.

Two objectives from the brief
01 · Human skills
Identify and map the essential human skills for human-AI co-creativity.
02 · Training program
Design a training program that empowers staff to integrate AI into their creative processes.

Essential human skills for co-creativity.

Objective 1 — the foundational human capabilities that make AI-augmented work technically efficient and deeply resonant with end users.

Creative intuition
Sensing what will resonate before the data confirms it — the spark AI cannot originate.
Empathy with end users
Understanding aspirations and pain points beyond what prompts and datasets can capture.
Critical evaluation
Judging AI output for accuracy, bias and fit — accountability stays with the human.
Curious questioning
Framing the right problem and the right prompt — the quality of the question shapes the answer.
Ethical judgement
Navigating risk, compliance and client trust where rules don't yet exist.
Collaborative iteration
Working with AI as a creative partner — refining, redirecting, co-authoring at pace.
These six capabilities frame the training program that follows.

Goals & strategic requirements.

How might we…

build a structured AI training program that equips business users with confidence in their critical thinking and technical ability — so that human-AI co-creativity becomes a genuine individual capability?

Synthesised from each team member's individual HMW statement in Assessment 2.

These three strategic requirements become the weighted criteria in our decision matrix.

Three solutions.

Three proposed program designs generated through divergent ideation and refined with Capgemini stakeholders.

Solution A
AI Centre of Excellence
A dedicated cross-functional team that owns AI training and enablement across the business.
How it works. A standing COE sets baseline productivity and critical-thinking metrics, then owns KPI uplift across the firm.

Why it works. The dual mandate (efficiency + human skill) needs an owner, not a project.

Trade-offs. Internal headcount; lower on structured training because the COE governs, not delivers.
Solution B
Bespoke Training
A training program co-designed with staff, tailored to Capgemini's actual workflows and client contexts.
How it works. Focus groups and workflow audits inform a Capgemini-specific curriculum, measured against company productivity.

Why it works. Co-design lands human skills in real work — closing the gap generic AI training leaves open.

Trade-offs. Design effort and time-to-launch; needs refresh as workflows evolve.
Solution C
Expert-led LMS with structured assessment
An LMS-hosted video series built with industry experts, with testing modules that measure capability uplift.
How it works. External AI experts build structured video modules and sandbox assessments hosted on a corporate LMS.

Why it works. Strong on structured delivery — measurable, auditable, scalable across the firm.

Trade-offs. Highest content cost; risks "tick-box" delivery without an internal owner driving improvement.

Weighting rationale: Capability (40%) carries the heaviest weight because it most directly measures the dual mandate's core promise — human-AI co-creativity as an individual skill. Training structure and confidence (30% each) are essential enablers. B narrowly leads on balance. A scores highest on capability. C delivers structured content efficiently but underperforms on the deeper outcomes — pointing to A + B as the recommendation, with C as enabling infrastructure.

Next steps.

The conclusion
Mindset shift
True digital transformation requires more than new tools — it requires a culture that treats AI as a co-creator.
Structured training
A 6-month LMS program directly addresses staff hesitation by building technical ability and critical thinking.
Sustained adoption
An AI Centre of Excellence ensures continuous, safe and industry-relevant AI adoption beyond the rollout.
Core contributions to Capgemini
Risk mitigation
Equips staff to confidently manage AI limitations — hallucinations, automation bias and data privacy.
Capability building
Transforms human-AI co-creation into a genuine, measurable individual capability across the firm.
Strategic alignment
Directly supports the goal of delivering proactive, empathetic, and secure services to public sector clients.
