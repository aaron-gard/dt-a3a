Understanding the problem.

AI marks the Fourth Industrial Revolution. What distinguishes it from previous shifts is a dual transformation — it amplifies productivity and ignites an intelligence revolution that reshapes human roles and the competencies required to thrive.

For Capgemini, the value of AI lies not in replacing people but in augmenting creativity. Merging human intuition with AI's computational power unlocks novel solutions for end users — but only if staff are equipped to lead that integration.

The dual mandate
Harness AI's automation and efficiency while cultivating the irreplaceable human skills that keep outcomes resonant for end users.
Human-AI co-creativity
The integration of human and AI capabilities to collaboratively design products, processes and services that address end users' aspirations and pain points.
Two objectives from the brief
1. Identify and map the essential human skills for human-AI co-creativity.
2. Design a training program that empowers staff to integrate AI into their creative processes.
How might we…

build a structured AI training program that equips business users with confidence in their critical thinking and technical ability — so that human-AI co-creativity becomes a genuine individual capability?

Essential human skills for co-creativity.

Objective 1 — the foundational human capabilities that make AI-augmented work technically efficient and deeply resonant with end users.

Creative intuition
Sensing what will resonate before the data confirms it — the spark AI cannot originate.
Empathy with end users
Understanding aspirations and pain points beyond what prompts and datasets can capture.
Critical evaluation
Judging AI output for accuracy, bias and fit — accountability stays with the human.
Curious questioning
Framing the right problem and the right prompt — the quality of the question shapes the answer.
Ethical judgement
Navigating risk, compliance and client trust where rules don't yet exist.
Collaborative iteration
Working with AI as a creative partner — refining, redirecting, co-authoring at pace.
These six capabilities frame the training program that follows.

Three alternatives for Capgemini to consider.

Generated through divergent ideation, evaluated against three weighted criteria the team agreed in workshop. Solution B narrowly leads, with Solution A close behind — our recommendation is the integrated program (A + B), with C as an enabling delivery channel.

Solution A
AI Centre of Excellence
A dedicated cross-functional team that owns AI training and enablement across the business.
How it works: Standing COE of cross-functional leaders sets baseline productivity and critical-thinking metrics, then owns KPI uplift across the firm.

Why it works: Embeds accountability and continuity — the dual mandate (efficiency + human skill) needs an owner, not a project.

Trade-offs: Internal headcount cost; lower scoring on "structured training" because the COE governs rather than delivers content.

From AI Centre of Excellence to continuous engagement.

To successfully transition Capgemini staff from viewing AI as a basic productivity tool to a trusted collaborative partner, we propose a comprehensive 6-month implementation plan. This strategy integrates the establishment of an AI Center of Excellence (COE), the development of a bespoke curriculum, and the deployment of a structured Learning Management System (LMS).

6.1 · Partnership establishment (Months 1–2)
Capgemini will form an AI Center of Excellence (COE) of cross-functional leaders to own the training and AI enablement strategy. Externally, the COE will partner with industry-leading AI experts and procure a robust LMS tailored for corporate upskilling. Baseline performance and productivity metrics will be set with clear KPIs to measure improvements in staff critical thinking and technical capabilities.
6.2 · Workflow analysis and curriculum (Months 2–3)
Rather than a generic off-the-shelf solution, the training must be deeply relevant to Capgemini's specific operational menu. The COE and external experts will collaborate with staff via focus groups to analyse current bottlenecks and design an industry-specific curriculum. This phase ensures the resulting video series and course materials directly address staff concerns, demystify AI, and provide clear, compliant guidance on human-AI co-creation.
6.3 · System implementation and training (Months 4–5)
With the curriculum designed, the program moves to deployment. The structured video series and interactive modules will be launched via the LMS, made accessible to all staff. Training will include practical sandbox exercises and tests evaluating both technical ability and critical thinking. Staff will learn how to prompt effectively, verify AI outputs, and confidently rely on AI for complex workflows.
6.4 · Engagement and continuous improvement (Month 6 +)
Training is not a one-off event but a continuous cultural shift. The COE will drive ongoing internal engagement campaigns to celebrate successful human-AI co-creation wins and gather continuous feedback from staff to refine the program. Effectiveness will be continuously measured against the initial KPIs, tracking company-wide productivity to ensure the training translates into genuine individual capability.
